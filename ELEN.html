ELEN – Electronics and Embedded Systems

The Electronics and Embedded Systems pathway (ÉSE) of the Bachelor's Degree in Electrical Engineering and Industrial Computing (GEII) focuses on the analysis, design and development of electronic systems. Students learn how electronic devices work and how to integrate, program, install and maintain them.

This specialization combines scientific knowledge with practical technical skills. Students work with electronic components, circuits, measuring instruments and computer-based tools to understand, develop and test electronic systems.

These technologies are used in many fields, including robotics, telecommunications, connected devices, transport and industrial equipment.

What Will You Study in This Specialization?

Electronic Circuits

Students learn how electronic circuits work and how their components interact. They study the principles used to design, assemble and test electronic circuits for different applications.

Analogue and Digital Electronics

Analogue electronics deals with signals that vary continuously, while digital electronics uses information represented by discrete values. Students learn how these circuits operate and how they are used in electronic devices and systems.

Sensors and Instrumentation

Sensors detect physical quantities such as temperature, pressure, position or light. Students learn how these measurements can be collected and used by electronic systems. Instrumentation helps them measure, observe and analyse the behaviour of a system.

Embedded Systems

An embedded system combines electronic hardware and software to perform a specific task. Students learn how to integrate and program these systems, which are found in devices such as robots, vehicles, connected objects and industrial equipment.

Programming and Electronic Control

Programming allows electronic systems to process information and perform operations. Students learn to develop and test programs that interact with electronic hardware and control the behaviour of embedded devices.

Communication and Signal Processing

Electronic systems often need to exchange information or process signals from their environment. Students discover how information can be acquired, processed and transmitted between different devices.

Testing, Integration and Maintenance

Students learn to assemble electronic components, integrate hardware and software, perform tests and identify faults. These skills help ensure that electronic equipment operates correctly and can be maintained when necessary.

How Are the Courses Taught?

Lectures and Tutorials (TD)

Lectures introduce the scientific and technical concepts needed to understand electronic systems. Tutorials help students apply this knowledge through exercises, calculations and technical problems.

Practical Laboratory Sessions (TP)

During laboratory sessions, students work with electronic components, circuits and measuring instruments. They assemble circuits, take measurements, test their designs and analyse the results.

Projects and Practical Assignments

Projects allow students to combine electronics, programming and testing to develop technical solutions. They may work individually or in teams to design, build and improve a prototype. These activities develop problem-solving, teamwork and project management skills.

Which Industries Use These Skills?

Electronics and embedded systems are used in many professional sectors. These include robotics, telecommunications, transport, industrial automation, connected devices, medical equipment and electronic instrumentation.

Depending on their role and experience, graduates may contribute to electronic design, prototype development, system integration, programming, testing and maintenance. They can work alongside technicians and engineers to develop and maintain electronic equipment.

Skills and Competencies

The GEII degree develops skills in designing, testing and maintaining technical systems. The ELEN pathway places particular emphasis on electronic systems and embedded technologies.
