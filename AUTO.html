AUTO – Automation and Industrial Computing

The Automation and Industrial Computing pathway (AII) of the Bachelor's Degree in Electrical Engineering and Industrial Computing (GEII) focuses on the automation and control of industrial processes. Students learn how to install, program and operate automated systems used in industrial production.

This specialization combines electrical engineering, programming, industrial computing and control systems. Students learn how machines communicate, how automated equipment operates and how industrial processes can be monitored and controlled.

Automation is used to improve production, ensure repeatable operations, monitor equipment and help maintain the performance and safety of industrial systems.

What Will You Study in This Specialization?

Industrial Automation

Industrial automation uses control systems to operate machines and production processes with limited manual intervention. Students learn how to analyse an automated process and understand the relationship between sensors, controllers and actuators.

Programmable Logic Controllers (PLCs)

PLCs are industrial computers used to control machines and production equipment. Students learn how to program controllers, manage inputs and outputs, and develop sequences of operations for automated systems.

Robotics

Industrial robots can perform tasks such as handling objects, assembling components and carrying out repetitive operations. Students discover how robots are programmed and integrated into automated production systems.

Sensors and Actuators

Sensors collect information about a machine or its environment, such as position, temperature or presence. Actuators perform actions, for example by moving a mechanism or operating a valve. Students learn how these components work together in an automated system.

Human-Machine Interfaces and Supervision

Human-machine interfaces allow operators to interact with industrial equipment. Supervisory systems display information about a process, monitor machine status and help operators detect problems or adjust settings.

Industrial Networks and Communication

Industrial equipment needs to exchange information to operate as a coordinated system. Students learn about industrial communication networks and how controllers, sensors, robots and supervisory systems share data.

Industrial Computing and Programming

Programming is used to control equipment, process data and manage automated operations. Students develop their ability to write, test and improve programs used in industrial applications.

Industrial Vision Systems

Industrial vision systems use cameras and image processing to inspect products, identify objects or check the quality of a manufactured item. These systems can help automate inspection and improve quality control.

Testing, Integration and Maintenance

Students learn to integrate automated equipment, test its operation and identify faults. They develop the skills needed to check that a system works correctly and to help maintain it in operational condition.
